## 1 What the data forced us to do

`train.csv` holds days 1–19 of every month of 2011–2012 and `test.csv` the 20th of every month, so the hidden test is whole unseen days inside the same two years. Three findings shaped every later choice. **(a) The signal is hour × day type:** working days have two commute peaks, other days one midday hump; a model with one hour profile cannot fit both. **(b) Demand grew strongly** from 2011 to 2012 and is right-skewed with spread growing with level, so we fit a transformed count throughout (Section 2) and always report R² and RMSE on bikes after transforming back. **(c) Several columns are copies:** `atemp` of `temp` (r = {{p4.column_verdicts.atemp.numbers.max_abs_corr.value:.3f}}), `season` of `mnth`, `workingday` of `weekday` and `holiday` (exactly), and `yr`, `instant` and the date all measure time. Quirks: humidity is 0 on one day (sensor failure, replaced by the training median), weather situation 4 occurs once (merged into 3), `cnt` is never 0 and night hours are the ones missing, so our models never see a zero-demand hour.

**Splits.** One seeded 80/20 split ({{p1.n_train}} / {{p1.n_val}} rows) gives every reported score and every tuning decision. Scalers, the humidity fill, expansions, every model fit and the class thresholds use the training rows only; every keep-or-drop decision (back-transform, degree, target level, penalties, column verdicts and therefore the surviving feature subset) is made on the validation set, which the task allows as tuning. Two further estimates are shown next to the validation score, never reported as "validation", and used as a check on every choice (in one place for more: the Phase 4 tie-break of Section 5): five folds of whole held-out days inside the training portion (fixed by the calendar, no seed), and the one chronological split of Phase 3. `test.csv` is used only for the final predictions.

## 2 Phase 1: gradient descent

**Representation.** 35 standardised columns: 23 hour dummies, `workingday`, `holiday`, two weather dummies, `temp`, `hum`, `windspeed`, a trend in days and two day-of-year sine/cosine pairs from `dteday`. Hour as one number gives validation R² {{p1.hour_encoding_ablation.numeric.val_r2:.3f}}, six harmonics {{p1.hour_encoding_ablation.harmonics6.val_r2:.3f}}, dummies {{p1.hour_encoding_ablation.one_hot.val_r2:.3f}}; we kept the dummies. The copies listed above stay out until Phase 4 so that the weights are unique (condition number {{p1.condition_number:.1f}}).

**Learning rate and stopping.** For the MSE loss gradient descent is stable only below 2/λ_max of XᵀX/n. We compute λ_max = {{p1.lambda_max:.3f}} and use half the bound, lr = {{p1.lr:.4f}}, starting from zeros. Sweep at fixed data and start: at 0.001 and 0.01 of the bound the run has not finished after {{p1.lr_sweep.0.iterations}} iterations (stall); at 0.1 it needs {{p1.lr_sweep.2.iterations}}; ours {{p1.lr_sweep.3.iterations}}; at 1.05 the loss explodes after {{p1.lr_sweep.5.iterations}} (Figure 1a). We stop when the relative loss change is below 1e-10 and the gradient norm below 1e-6: either test alone can fire on a plateau. The final weights match the closed-form solution to within 1e-5 (used only as a check).

**Target transform.** We fit z = ((cnt + 1)^λ − 1)/λ with λ = {{p1.target_power}}; λ → 0 is the log and λ = 1 the raw count. Our first pass used the log. It treats an error of four bikes at 3 am like an error of two hundred at 5 pm, while R² on bikes is decided by the busy hours; the raw count loses the multiplicative structure instead. With the Phase 1 design, validation R² is {{p1.target_power_table.0.val_r2:.4f}} for the log, {{p1.target_power_table.2.val_r2:.4f}} at 0.1, {{p1.target_power_table.5.val_r2:.4f}} at 0.3 (the best for this small model) and {{p1.target_power_table.7.val_r2:.4f}} for raw counts. The exponent must be the same in every phase, or Phase 2 could not start from Phase 1's weights, and the best exponent shrinks as the model gains structure; we fix 0.1, the value the final design prefers (Phase 3 re-checks it), knowing it is not this phase's own optimum. This choice comes from the first pass, not from Phase 1 alone.

**Back-transform.** Chosen on validation between no factor (R² {{p1.backtransform.candidates.none.val_r2:.4f}}; predictions average {{p1.backtransform.candidates.none.val_mean_ratio:.2f}} of the true mean) and a least-squares factor s = Σ(cnt+1)q/Σq² = {{p1.backtransform.candidates.ls.factor:.3f}} on q = (λz + 1)^(1/λ) (R² {{p1.backtransform.candidates.ls.val_r2:.4f}}). We keep the factor for all phases. In the first pass Duan's smearing, the textbook correction for a log model, was the worst option ({{first_pass.p1.none_val_r2:.3f}} → {{first_pass.p1.duan_val_r2:.3f}}): the large log-errors sit in quiet night hours while the factor inflates the peaks.

**Result.** Validation R² {{p1.val_r2:.4f}} (95% bootstrap interval {{p1.val_bootstrap.lo:.3f}}–{{p1.val_bootstrap.hi:.3f}}), RMSE {{p1.val_rmse:.1f}}; train R² {{p1.train_r2:.4f}}; first pass {{first_pass.p1.val_r2:.4f}}. The residuals by hour differ sharply between day types, which is what Phase 2 addresses. *Surprise:* we expected the milder transform to bring the back-transform factor closer to 1; it moved from {{first_pass.p1.backtransform_factor:.3f}} to {{p1.backtransform.candidates.ls.factor:.3f}}.

**Bonus: asymmetric cost.** With under-prediction three times as costly, we minimise L(w) = (1/n) Σ cᵢ(ŷᵢ − yᵢ)², ŷᵢ = q(xᵢ·w) − 1 with q(η) = (λη + 1)^(1/λ), cᵢ = 3 if ŷᵢ < yᵢ and 1 otherwise, on bikes because the cost is paid in bikes. Since q′(η) = (λη + 1)^(1/λ − 1) = q(η)^(1−λ), the chain rule gives ∇L = (2/n) Σ cᵢ(ŷᵢ − yᵢ) q(ηᵢ)^(1−λ) xᵢ (for the log, λ → 0, this is exp(ηᵢ)xᵢ); cᵢ jumps only where the residual is zero and adds no term. The loss is not quadratic, so we use gradient descent with a backtracking step from the Phase 1 weights ({{p1.bonus.iterations}} iterations, "{{p1.bonus.stop_reason}}"). Predictions shift up by a factor {{p1.bonus.mean_shift_ratio:.2f}}; the share of under-predicted validation hours falls from {{p1.bonus.val.mse_model.under_share:.2f}} to {{p1.bonus.val.asym_model.under_share:.2f}}, the asymmetric cost from {{p1.bonus.val.mse_model.sq_cost:.0f}} to {{p1.bonus.val.asym_model.sq_cost:.0f}}, and plain RMSE rises from {{p1.bonus.val.mse_model.rmse:.1f}} to {{p1.bonus.val.asym_model.rmse:.1f}}. As a control we fitted the same bike-scale loss with k = 1: fitting on bikes alone shifts predictions by {{bonus_control.shift_from_fitting_on_bikes:.3f}} (under-predicted share {{bonus_control.val.k1_model.under_share:.2f}}), and the asymmetry adds a further {{bonus_control.shift_from_asymmetry:.3f}}, so about two thirds of the shift is the operator's asymmetry. (In this comparison the Phase 1 model is used without its back-transform factor.)

## 3 Phase 2: polynomial regression

**Initialisation.** The 35 base columns keep the Phase 1 scaler from `p1.json`; new columns get their own; the weight vector is Phase 1's with zeros in the new slots. The first Phase 2 loss therefore equals the last Phase 1 loss ({{p2.init_loss:.6f}}, difference below 1e-9, asserted in the notebook).

**What we expanded.** Only where Phase 1's residuals showed structure: the 23 degree-2 products `workingday × hour` (without them the expanded model reaches {{p2.block_ablation.without_wd_x_hr.val_r2:.4f}}) and powers of `temp`. Adding powers of `hum` or `windspeed` changes validation R² by less than our tolerance ({{p2.power_col_sweep.0.val_r2:.4f}} → {{p2.power_col_sweep.1.val_r2:.4f}} → {{p2.power_col_sweep.2.val_r2:.4f}}). `trend` is never raised to a power: a polynomial in time bends away outside the observed dates.

**Degree.** Rule fixed before the run: the smallest degree within 0.001 of the best. Validation R² by degree 1–4: {{p2.degree_sweep.0.val_r2:.4f}}, {{p2.degree_sweep.1.val_r2:.4f}}, {{p2.degree_sweep.2.val_r2:.4f}}, {{p2.degree_sweep.3.val_r2:.4f}} → degree {{p2.degree}}.

**Result.** {{p2.power_col_sweep.0.n_features}} weights, {{p2.iterations}} iterations at half the bound (lr {{p2.lr:.4f}}), validation R² {{p2.val_r2:.4f}}, RMSE {{p2.val_rmse:.1f}}; gain over Phase 1 {{p2.paired_vs_p1.delta:.3f}} (paired interval {{p2.paired_vs_p1.lo:.3f}}–{{p2.paired_vs_p1.hi:.3f}}); train R² {{p2.train_r2:.4f}}; first pass {{first_pass.p2.val_r2:.4f}}. *Surprise:* optimisation stayed easy (condition number {{p2.condition_number:.0f}}), because powers and products are built from standardised columns.

**The time question.** We train before {{p3.cut_date}} and validate after (a quarter of the file, half a seasonal cycle, each month seen at least once in training). For Phase 2 the three estimates are {{p3.estimates.seeded.r2:.3f}} (seeded), {{p3.estimates.day_holdout.r2:.3f}} (held-out days) and {{p3.estimates.chrono.r2:.3f}} (chronological); for the target {{p3.estimates_target.seeded.r2:.3f}}, {{p3.estimates_target.day_holdout.r2:.3f}} and {{p3.estimates_target.chrono.r2:.3f}}. We separated the two possible causes of a gap. Sharing days between training and validation is worth nothing to these models ({{p3.gaps.anchor.leakage:.4f}}, against a fold spread of {{p3.estimates.day_holdout.sd:.3f}}): a linear model with a few hundred weights cannot recognise an individual day. A tree model we tried outside the pipeline can, and scores {{diagnostics.tree_model.seeded_minus_days:.3f}} higher on the seeded split than on held-out days, so the warning in the brief is real; our model class is too stiff to profit. What gap there is, {{p3.gaps.anchor.drift:.3f}} for Phase 2 and {{p3.gaps.target.drift:.3f}} for the target, is drift: after the cut mean demand is {{p3.chrono_detail.anchor.mean_cnt_late:.0f}} against {{p3.chrono_detail.anchor.mean_cnt_early:.0f}} bikes and the trend overshoots it (target predictions average {{p3.chrono_detail.target.mean_ratio:.2f}} times the truth). **We trust the chronological estimate for future periods**; for our hidden test, which lies inside the observed period, held-out days are the closest match. The diagnosis does not change in kind, the simpler models are worse on every split, but the chronological split is the one that punishes the levels above the target ({{p3.ladder.6.chrono.r2:.3f}} at C6, {{p3.ladder.8.chrono.r2:.3f}} at C8), so it limits how far up we go. *Biggest lesson of the second pass:* with the log target the same design scored {{first_pass.p3.target.chrono:.3f}} chronologically and over-shot the later months by a factor {{first_pass.p3.chrono_mean_ratio_target:.2f}}. Most of what we had called drift was our own target: a straight trend on the log scale is exponential growth.

**Target complexity.** Rule: the simplest level within {{p3.plateau_tol}} validation R² of the best → {{p3.target_complexity.level}}, {{p3.target_complexity.n_features}} weights. Two disclosures. Our first rule was plain "highest validation R²"; on its first run it preferred a level 140 weights larger on a difference of 0.0005, so we replaced it with the tolerance rule we already used for the degree. And level C6 was added after a residual analysis of C5 (error concentrated in working-day rush hours and in rain): powers of humidity, wind terms, `workingday × hour × temp`, and the weather situation of the previous one and three hours (inputs only). It was accepted because it gained on held-out days in every fold and on the chronological split; having been designed after looking at validation errors, its validation score is the most optimistic number in this report. **Exponent check.** On the target design validation R² is {{p3.target_power_check.0.seeded_r2:.4f}} for the log, {{p3.target_power_check.1.seeded_r2:.4f}} at 0.05, {{p3.target_power_check.2.seeded_r2:.4f}} at 0.1, {{p3.target_power_check.3.seeded_r2:.4f}} at 0.15 and {{p3.target_power_check.6.seeded_r2:.4f}} at 0.5; held-out days agree ({{p3.target_power_check.0.day_block_r2:.4f}} → {{p3.target_power_check.2.day_block_r2:.4f}}). ## 5 Phase 4: regularization and column verdicts

**Search, informed by Phase 3.** The model we carry forward is not over-fit, so the grid must reach practically zero and include the unpenalised fit as reference. All three methods use the same standardised design (the target plus the held-back copies `atemp`, `yr`, `instant`, `season`, `mnth`, so every original column is present), the same rows, 40 log-spaced penalties each (Ridge {{p4.grids.l2.min:.0e}}–{{p4.grids.l2.max:.0f}}; Lasso and Elastic Net from the all-zero penalty down to a ten-thousandth of it; l1_ratio in six steps from 0.1 to 0.95), the same rule (highest validation R² on bikes) and the same checks. Ridge is closed-form; Lasso and Elastic Net are our own coordinate descent; our unit tests compare all three with a reference library (agreement better than 1e-3). {{p4.not_converged.count}} Lasso fits at one large penalty on this design, none of them chosen, used all {{p4.solver.max_sweeps}} sweeps without meeting our tolerance: exactly duplicated columns make coordinate descent crawl; on the survivors every fit converged.

Three things in this table surprised us. Lasso's choice between copies is arbitrary and once backwards: it keeps `atemp`, which costs nothing to drop, in most resampled fits and never keeps `instant`, which alone explains {{p4.column_verdicts.instant.numbers.solo_r2:.3f}}; so "Lasso set it to zero" was evidence for us but never the verdict. `dteday` is useful only as a group: its copies can replace it completely (cost alone {{p4.column_verdicts.dteday.numbers.drop_alone.delta:.4f}}, with them {{p4.column_verdicts.dteday.numbers.drop_group.delta:.3f}}). And `windspeed` is useful only since Phase 3 gave it a square and an interaction with temperature; as a single linear column the same rule had called it uninformative. A verdict judges a column as we represented it (Figure 2a). One apparent contradiction: `workingday` is an exact function of `weekday` and `holiday`, yet both `workingday` and `weekday` are useful. The copy is exact only for the column itself; each is missed through its own hour profile (`workingday × hour` separates holidays from ordinary weekdays hour by hour, `weekday × hour` separates Friday evenings and Saturdays from Sundays), while `holiday` on its own adds nothing once those are present.

**Survivors and final model (stage B).** We first remove every feature built from a column that is not useful ({{p4.survivor_counts.useful_design}} features remain), then run Lasso on that reduced design; a feature survives if its weight is non-zero at the chosen λ and it is selected in at least 0.6 of 50 Lasso fits on resampled training days: {{p4.survivor_counts.lasso_nonzero}} → {{p4.survivor_counts.stable}} features. Lasso removes almost nothing here, so the subset is decided almost entirely by the verdicts. To be exact about what is fitted where: every model, scaler and Lasso fit uses training rows only; which columns are kept is a decision scored on the validation set, like the degree and the penalties. (Our first version took the Lasso zeros before removing the copies; Lasso had let month dummies and `instant` stand in for date terms, the verdict step then removed the stand-ins, and the chronological score fell by about 0.013. We corrected the order.) The redundant columns add nothing: dropping `mnth` alone changes validation R² by {{p4.column_verdicts.mnth.numbers.drop_alone.delta:.4f}}, so the model is slightly better without it. We therefore repeated the same search on the survivors only and recommend among those: Ridge {{p4.final.methods.l2.val_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.l2.day_block_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.l2.chrono_r2:.3f}} (validation / held-out days / chronological), Lasso {{p4.final.methods.l1.val_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.l1.day_block_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.l1.chrono_r2:.3f}}, Elastic Net {{p4.final.methods.enet.val_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.enet.day_block_r2:.4f}} / {{p4.final.methods.enet.chrono_r2:.3f}}. They are again tied on validation, and Ridge's best penalty is the smallest on our grid ({{p4.final.methods.l2.lambda:.6f}}), i.e. none: the unpenalised fit on the survivors scores {{p4.final.unregularised.val_r2:.4f}}. Once the copies are gone, nothing is left for a penalty to fix; our rule (within noise on validation, then best on held-out days; this tie-break is the one decision not made on the validation set alone, and here it picks the method that is also best on validation) recommends **{{p4.recommended.method}}**, which keeps {{p4.final.methods.l1.n_nonzero}} of the {{p4.survivor_counts.stable}} surviving features, with λ = {{p4.recommended.lambda:.6f}}, l1_ratio {{p4.recommended.l1_ratio}}: validation R² {{p4.recommended.val_r2:.4f}}, RMSE {{p4.recommended.val_rmse:.1f}} (first pass: {{first_pass.p4.val_r2:.4f}} and {{first_pass.p4.val_rmse:.1f}}; held-out days {{first_pass.p4.day_block_r2:.4f}} → {{p4.recommended.day_block_r2:.4f}}, chronological {{first_pass.p4.chrono_r2:.3f}} → {{p4.recommended.chrono_r2:.3f}}). This second stage was added after the first Phase 4 run; all runs are in the git history.

## 6 Phase 5: logistic regression

**Label.** A single global threshold has an obvious flaw: "high" then just means rush hour. Under the global 75th percentile, hour dummies alone reach ROC-AUC {{p5.label_variants.0.auc_hour_only:.3f}} and the full model {{p5.label_variants.0.auc_full:.3f}}: impressive and useless, the operator already has a clock. A threshold per (day type, hour) fixes the clock but not the calendar: {{p5.label_variants.1.pos_val_2011:.3f}} of 2011 validation hours are positive against {{p5.label_variants.1.pos_val_2012:.3f}} of 2012, and the trend alone reaches {{p5.label_variants.1.auc_trend_only:.3f}}. **Our label:** `cnt` above the 75th percentile of training hours with the same year, day type and hour ({{p5.threshold_rule.n_cells}} thresholds from the training rows, at least {{p5.threshold_rule.min_cell_n}} hours each): "busier than three quarters of comparable hours", the case where the normal allocation runs short. Positives are {{p5.class_balance.train:.3f}} of training and {{p5.class_balance.val:.3f}} of validation hours ({{p5.class_balance.val_by_year.0:.3f}} / {{p5.class_balance.val_by_year.1:.3f}} by year); hour alone is at chance ({{p5.label_variants.2.auc_hour_only:.3f}}) and trend alone at {{p5.label_variants.2.auc_trend_only:.3f}}.

**Model.** Our own logistic regression (gradient descent on the log-loss, lr = 1/(λ_max/4 + l2), gradient-checked) on the {{p4.survivor_counts.stable}} Phase 4 survivors only (asserted). A small ridge term is chosen by validation ROC-AUC: l2 = {{p5.l2}}, {{p5.iterations}} iterations.

**Metrics for a 1:3 class balance.** Always answering "normal" is right {{p5.class_balance.majority_accuracy:.3f}} of the time, so accuracy flatters; we read it with F1, precision, recall, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC. **Threshold.** A missed high-demand hour (empty docks) costs more than a false alarm; with the 3:1 ratio of the bonus the cost-minimising cut-off of a calibrated model is 1/(1+3) = 0.25.

The chain is checked in code: each artifact stores the hash of the one it consumed; Phase 2's first loss equals Phase 1's last; Phase 3's anchor is Phase 2's design; Phase 4's design is Phase 3's target; Phase 5's features are a subset of Phase 4's survivors. The story the numbers tell is one of bias: each step that added structure the data really has (day-type hour profiles, then weather by hour, then weekday profiles, then weather detail) raised validation R² from {{p1.val_r2:.2f}} to {{p4.recommended.val_r2:.2f}}, while penalties, the classical cure for variance, changed almost nothing. The one exception to "add structure" was our own target: replacing the log by a milder transform was worth more on unseen months than any feature.

**Submitted model.** {{p6.model.method}} on the Phase 4 survivors with the validated hyper-parameters, weights refitted on training + validation rows ({{p6.n_fit_rows}} rows; the Phase 3 learning curve of the target is still rising at full size) with scaler and humidity fill kept as fitted on the training rows. Refit and validated model agree on the test rows (correlation {{p6.a_vs_b_on_test.corr:.4f}}, mean ratio {{p6.a_vs_b_on_test.mean_ratio:.3f}}). Sanity of the {{p6.n_test_rows}} predictions: none negative, mean {{p6.pred.mean:.1f}} bikes (training mean {{p6.train_cnt_mean:.1f}}), per (day type, hour) cell between {{p6.profile_ratio.min:.2f}} and {{p6.profile_ratio.max:.2f}} times the training profile.

**What we do not claim.** (1) Validation scores are slightly optimistic: the exponent, back-transform, degree, target level and penalties were all chosen on the same {{p1.n_val}} rows. Rules and designs were changed after first results: the Phase 3 target rule, one ladder level, the order of the selection step, the second stage of Phase 4, and, in the second pass, the target transform itself. Held-out days and the chronological split are our guard, but they are not untouched either: both were looked at when we accepted those changes, and the two weather-memory columns are built from the weather (never the counts) of neighbouring rows before the split. For unseen future months the least optimistic figure we have is the chronological {{p4.recommended.chrono_r2:.3f}}, not {{p4.recommended.val_r2:.3f}}. (2) The trend is a straight line on the transformed scale: fine inside 2011–2012, still somewhat too steep beyond. (3) The model has never seen a zero-demand hour, a new weather regime, or a holiday type absent from training; the Phase 5 thresholds exist only for the two observed years. (4) What we tried and left out (all in `exp/v2/`, none in the pipeline): {{diagnostics.feature_blocks_tested}} more groups of input-only features, of which no weather or hour group moved held-out days by more than {{diagnostics.weather_blocks_max_abs_days_delta:.4f}}; separate levels per calendar month, which gain {{diagnostics.year_month_levels.held_out_days_delta:.3f}} on held-out days and lose {{diagnostics.year_month_levels.chrono_delta:.3f}} chronologically; and observation weights, which gain {{diagnostics.observation_weights.held_out_days_delta:.4f}} on held-out days at a paired standard error of {{diagnostics.observation_weights.paired_se:.4f}}, below our two-standard-error rule. A diagnostic tree model scores {{diagnostics.tree_model.held_out_days:.3f}} on held-out days and {{diagnostics.tree_model.chrono:.3f}} chronologically; our final model is at {{p4.recommended.day_block_r2:.3f}} and {{p4.recommended.chrono_r2:.3f}}. The remaining error is mostly a whole-day offset we cannot observe.
